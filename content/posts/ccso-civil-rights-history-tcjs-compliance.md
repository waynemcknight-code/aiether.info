A 2018 federal civil rights lawsuit named the Colorado County Jail and four of its supervisors as defendants. One of those supervisors remains employed there today. Meanwhile, the jail continues to accumulate documented compliance failures despite having received $2.2 million in federal American Rescue Plan Act funds specifically designated for jail compliance and infrastructure.

---

## 2018 Federal Civil Rights Suit — *Murphy v. Colorado County Jail*

On April 20, 2018, a civil rights complaint was removed to the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of Texas (Case No. 4:18-cv-01246) naming the following defendants:

- **Colorado County Jail**
- **Sheriff R.H. "Curly" Wied**
- **Jail Lt. Tracey Lewis**
- **Cpt. Tommy Richter**
- **Sgt. Brett Vasek**
- **Christopher Jones**

The case was classified under Nature of Suit 440 — Civil Rights: Other. It originated in the 61st Judicial District of Harris County, Texas (State Case No. 201816544) before being removed to federal court by the defendants.

The case was terminated on May 31, 2018 — approximately six weeks after filing — following a dismissal. The dismissal was procedural: mail sent to the pro se plaintiff was returned undeliverable. The county was never required to defend the claims on the merits.

**Sheriff Wied has since been replaced by Sheriff Justin Lindemann. Cpt. Tommy Richter has retired. Lt. Tracey Lewis remains employed at the Colorado County Sheriff's Office.**

The retention of a supervisor with prior federal civil rights exposure — without any public accounting of what conduct was alleged or what corrective action, if any, was taken — raises serious questions about institutional culture and oversight at CCSO.

## TCJS Compliance Failures

According to the Texas Commission on Jail Standards, the Colorado County Jail has a rated capacity of 99 beds. As of April 2026, the following deficiencies have been documented or publicly acknowledged:

- **Rust** documented as a compliance issue as recently as 2024
- **Out-of-compliance sliding doors**
- **Only one psychiatric cell** in a 99-bed facility
- **Only 10 female beds** — the county pays approximately $100 per day to house female inmates at other facilities due to this self-imposed capacity constraint
- **No space for additional sheriff's office staff**

Sheriff Lindemann stated publicly that the facility has gone "30 years without any" upgrades.

The jail recently passed its most recent TCJS inspection despite these documented deficiencies. The jail is not operating at capacity — as of April 2026, it housed approximately 60 inmates (roughly 60 percent of rated capacity), down from 80 this time last year.

## $2.2 Million in Federal Funds — No Accounting

Colorado County received $2.2 million in federal Coronavirus State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds (SLFRF) under the American Rescue Plan Act, specifically designated for the Colorado County Jail's compliance and infrastructure needs.

That money came with federal compliance requirements and an audit window that runs through **2030**. The federal government retains the authority to demand accountability for every dollar of it.

Three years into the federal grant period, the Sheriff cited rust as an ongoing compliance issue. The county has offered no public explanation of what the $2.2 million was spent on, or why documented compliance failures persist after those funds were received.

In April 2026, the Commissioners Court approved an architectural assessment for a proposed new justice center. Before any additional public funds are committed, the county owes taxpayers a full accounting of what happened to the $2.2 million already received.

---

## Pattern

Colorado County has a documented pattern of accepting federal funds, failing to achieve compliance, and requesting additional resources when the problems persist. The 2018 civil rights lawsuit against jail supervisors was dismissed on a technicality — not on the merits. At least one named supervisor remains on staff. The jail continues to accumulate TCJS deficiencies. And $2.2 million in federal compliance funds remains unaccounted for.

These are not isolated failures. They are a pattern. And patterns have legal significance.
