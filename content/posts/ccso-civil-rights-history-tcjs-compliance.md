Two separate federal civil rights lawsuits — one filed in 2016, one in 2018 — named the Colorado County Sheriff's Office as a defendant in cases involving excessive force, deliberate indifference to medical needs, and failure to supervise. At least one supervisor named in the 2018 suit remains employed at CCSO today. The same institution has accumulated documented compliance failures while $2.2 million in federal funds designated for jail infrastructure remains unaccounted for.

---

## 2016 — *Ortiz v. Singer* — Excessive Force and Monell Policy Claim

**Case No. 24591, 25th District Court, Colorado County; removed as 4:16-cv-03708, S.D. Tex.**

On December 7, 2014, Barbara Ortiz was a passenger in Alfred Singer's vehicle in San Felipe, Texas. Singer failed to stop for a traffic violation, triggering a multi-agency high-speed pursuit. **Deputy William Lund of the Colorado County Sheriff's Office** joined the pursuit.

During the chase, a DPS trooper fired **13 rounds** into the passenger side door of the vehicle at highway speed. Ortiz was struck in the head and in the back. She was pregnant with twins at the time of the shooting. She lost one of the twins as a result of her injuries.

Barbara Ortiz filed suit in the 25th Judicial District Court of Colorado County on December 1, 2016, asserting two counts directly against **Colorado County and the Colorado County Sheriff's Office**:

**Count II — Excessive Force (42 U.S.C. § 1983):** CCSO and Deputy Lund continued the pursuit with "conscious indifference and reckless disregard for the safety of others," in violation of the Fourth Amendment.

**Count III — Official Policy or Custom (42 U.S.C. § 1983 — Monell):** Colorado County and CCSO were liable under § 1983 because their "failure to properly supervise, screen, discipline, transfer, counsel or otherwise control officers who are known, or who should have been known, to engage in the use of excessive force caused the deprivation of Plaintiff's constitutional rights" and "was inflicted pursuant to an official policy or custom of Defendants."

The case was removed to federal court by Austin County and ultimately remanded back to the 25th District Court of Colorado County — the same court that handles civil matters originating in the county. Its resolution was not publicly reported.

## 2018 — *Murphy v. Colorado County Jail* — Deliberate Medical Indifference

**State Case No. 201816544, 61st District Court, Harris County; removed as 4:18-cv-01246, S.D. Tex.**

On March 9, 2018, Alvin Murphy was incarcerated at the Colorado County Jail in Columbus, Texas. In his sworn petition, he stated the following:

He suffered a seizure or loss of consciousness in his cell, causing blunt force trauma to his head and nose, visible bleeding, and a visible knot on the right side of his forehead from contact with the cement floor. He notified the Control Picket Operator (Ms. Meeks), Jail Nurse Heather, and Corporal Jones of his condition.

The response from jail staff:

- The nurse viewed him **through the cell door glass only** — never entering the cell
- An X-ray technician came and took an X-ray of his **neck only**, ignoring the head injury
- After Murphy reported continued loss of consciousness, extreme pain, blurry vision, and tremors — **jail staff refused further medical attention**
- The Control Picket Operator told him to **"just lay down and shut the hell up"**
- **The intercom was then turned off**
- Murphy was left isolated in a single cell, with no contact from approximately noon through 3:00 p.m., while continuing to lose consciousness and vision

He requested $100,000 in damages and a court order requiring the jail to have a doctor on site for medical emergencies.

Murphy filed suit on March 13, 2018 in Harris County. On April 20, 2018, the defendants — represented by **J. Eric Magee of Allison, Bass & Magee, L.L.P.** — removed the case to federal court and simultaneously filed an answer and motion to dismiss. The case was terminated May 31, 2018 after mail to the pro se plaintiff was returned undeliverable. **The county was never required to defend the allegations on the merits.**

**Named defendants:**
- Colorado County Jail
- Sheriff R.H. "Curly" Wied
- **Jail Lt. Tracey Lewis**
- Cpt. Tommy Richter
- **Sgt. Brett Vasek**
- Corporal Christopher Jones

**Sheriff Wied has since been replaced by Sheriff Justin Lindemann. Cpt. Tommy Richter has retired. Lt. Tracey Lewis remains employed at the Colorado County Sheriff's Office.**

## TCJS Compliance Failures

According to the Texas Commission on Jail Standards, the Colorado County Jail has a rated capacity of 99 beds. As of April 2026, the following deficiencies have been documented or publicly acknowledged:

- **Rust** documented as a compliance issue as recently as 2024
- **Out-of-compliance sliding doors**
- **Only one psychiatric cell** in a 99-bed facility
- **Only 10 female beds** — the county pays approximately $100 per day to house female inmates at other facilities due to this self-imposed configuration
- **No space for additional sheriff's office staff**

Sheriff Lindemann stated publicly that the facility has gone "30 years without any" upgrades. The jail recently passed its most recent TCJS inspection despite these documented deficiencies. As of April 2026 it housed approximately 60 inmates — 60 percent of rated capacity.

## $2.2 Million in Federal Funds — No Accounting

Colorado County received $2.2 million in federal Coronavirus State and Local Fiscal Recovery Funds (SLFRF) under the American Rescue Plan Act, specifically designated for Colorado County Jail compliance and infrastructure. That money came with compliance requirements and a federal audit window running through **2030**.

Three years into the grant period, the Sheriff cited rust as an active compliance issue. The county has offered no public explanation of what the $2.2 million was spent on or why documented failures persist. In April 2026, the Commissioners Court approved an architectural assessment for a proposed new justice center — without first accounting for the $2.2 million already received for the existing one.

## The Pattern

- **2014:** CCSO deputy participates in high-speed pursuit ending in passenger shot 13 times
- **2016:** Federal §1983 + Monell suit filed against CCSO for excessive force and failure to supervise
- **2018:** Federal §1983 suit filed against CCSO jail supervisors for deliberate medical indifference — allegations include intercom disabled, nurse refuses to enter cell, inmate told to "shut the hell up"
- **Both cases dismissed without merits review.** One named supervisor from 2018 remains employed.
- **2024:** Rust cited as active compliance deficiency — three years after $2.2M in federal compliance funds received
- **2026:** County seeks additional public funds for a new justice center

The same institution. The same supervisors. No documented corrective action. No public accounting.

These are not isolated failures. They are a pattern. And patterns have legal significance.
